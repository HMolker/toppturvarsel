A few days in one area

How it ranks: first an avalanche filter. Danger level is weighed against the tour's steepness (its difficulty rating), and against whether its aspect and elevation fall inside a problem the bulletin names. Tours that fail are excluded, not marked down. Then a score out of 100: fresh snow and surface 25 %, base 20 % (full marks from 100 cm), weather that day 25 % (with the hourly forecast: the best window inside the daylight, for as long as the tour takes), tour quality 20 %, fit 10 %. Fresh snow and surface are judged by the descent aspect: wind while it snowed, warming and the spring corn cycle. Days after the last bulletin reuse it and say so. This sorts what the bulletin says; read the bulletin before you go.

Proposals — ideas for later

Not built yet. Kept here so they are not forgotten.
  1. Travel time and road status. Real driving time from where you start instead of distance as the crow flies (openrouteservice, free key), and mountain passes and winter closures from Statens vegvesen (Datex II, needs registration) and Trafikverket (open API, needs a key). A tour behind a closed pass would then drop out of the plan by itself.
  2. Ground truth. Recent Regobs observations near each tour (snow surface, cracking, avalanche activity; check the licence and crediting again in December), webcams near the trailheads, and a trip log where you rate the day you actually had, so the score can be tuned against reality.
  3. Personal alerts. A push or email when a tour you would rate 75+ turns up within, say, four hours' drive, for a day you have marked as free. Builds on the planner score and, once it exists, on travel time.
  4. More mountains. The Pyrenees (Spain, France, Andorra) and North Macedonia, each with its own avalanche service and snow data. The country selector is already built for it.
  5. Tour editor that saves. The editor preview on this server, with a password, writing tracks, descriptions and photos straight into the data folder instead of a downloaded zip.
  6. Official EAWS pictograms. Swap the drawn problem icons for the EAWS originals if permission to use them is given.

Where the numbers come from

  • Avalanche danger, Norway: the Norwegian Avalanche Warning Service (NVE) via the Varsom API — 23 mainland regions plus Nordenskiöld Land, published daily before 16:00, 1 Dec – 31 May. Danger level, avalanche problems, mountain weather and the forecaster's summary of recent observations and avalanche activity. Data © NVE / Varsom.no.
  • Avalanche danger, Sweden: Naturvårdsverket's lavinprognoser.se. Sweden publishes no API, so this is read from the public page and is best-effort — if it can't be read, the page says so and links you to the bulletin rather than guessing. Swedish forecasts resume 11 December.
  • Snow depth and new snow: NVE's seNorge model grid (gts.nve.no), 1 km cells, daily at 06:00, sampled at each tour's own coordinates rather than a region centroid. The grid covers the Swedish mountains too, so both countries use it. New snow is the rise in modelled depth, which settlement makes conservative: 40 cm falling may read as +30 by morning.
  • Field observations: for Norway, the forecaster's own observation and avalanche-activity summary from the bulletin. Raw Regobs counts are available but off by default — see the README for why. Regobs data requires crediting both Regobs and the individual observer.
  • Ski resorts: Norway from Fnugg — lifts and slopes open, as reported by the resorts, refreshed hourly in season. Sweden from OpenStreetMap (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL) — position and website only, because no Swedish source publishes live lift status openly; those resorts show dashed "no status" icons, which do not mean closed.

Ratings

Quality (★1–5) and difficulty (1–5) are an editorial call, not an official grade. Roughly: 1 = rolling fjäll; 2 = sustained 25–30°; 3 = 30–35° with exposure or a crux; 4 = 35–45° faces and couloirs, no-fall sections; 5 = ski-mountaineering, rope/glacier/rappel likely. Coordinates and summit heights are approximate — they exist to put a pin in the right cirque and to sample the right grid cell, not to navigate by.

Reality check

A regional bulletin describes a region, not your slope. Modelled snow depth is a model, not a pit. Nothing here replaces terrain assessment, a partner, transceiver/shovel/probe, and the willingness to turn around.
