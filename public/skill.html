How this is measured

Kept every morning

For each 25 km cell holding a tour or a resort, the forecast for 1, 3, 5, 10 and 15 days ahead is written down. The same request brings back the last five days as analysed, which is what the older forecasts are then scored against.

Scored against normal, not against zero

An error of 2 °C means nothing on its own. Each score is the forecast's error measured against the error of simply saying "normal for the time of year", built from the observations collected here:
skill = 1 − forecast error ÷ error of saying "normal"
0 means no better than knowing the season. 1 means perfect.

The four, and the one

New snow (and whether 5 cm fell), the day's highest temperature, mean cloud (a bluebird day is under 30 %), and the day's strongest wind. All four is a weighted average — snow 40 %, temperature 25 %, wind 20 %, cloud 15 % — because they do not matter equally when you are choosing a day.

Nothing is shown too early

A cell needs 30 scored days before it shows a number, and enough observations for a climatology. Until then it stays blank. A 15-day forecast made today cannot be marked for a fortnight, so the long lead times fill in last.
