How this page works

  • Slope and runout, Norway: NVE's slope-angle map with avalanche runout zones (bratthet med utløp, 2024), from Kartverket's 1 m and 10 m terrain models. Green from 27°, yellow to red and purple above 30°, runout zones in three blues (short, medium, long: roughly one avalanche in two, four and twenty runs further). © NVE, CC BY 4.0.
  • Computed shading and the route: heights from Kartverket (Norway, 1 m / 10 m) or Copernicus GLO-90 (Sweden, 90 m) through this service. The map shading is worked out in your browser from a grid of 17 × 17 heights per map tile, so it gets finer as you zoom in; at low zoom it averages steep faces down. The route is measured every 25 m or more, each point with a small cross of heights around it, so its slope is the ground's, not the route's own gradient. In Sweden that cross spans 90 m cells and steep short faces read flatter than they are.
  • Suggested routes are the cheapest way on skins from start to end when steep ground, runout zones and today's problem slopes cost extra. They know nothing about cornices, glaciers, cliff bands smaller than a grid cell, forest, open water or the snow. Unverified: a line to look at, not to follow.
  • Service area: everything here covers 12 km around the tours and ski resorts in Fjällskred, so this box never becomes a free map or elevation service for the internet. A daily budget caps how many heights it asks for.

Slope angle is only one part of avalanche terrain. Read the bulletin, and look at the slope you are on.
